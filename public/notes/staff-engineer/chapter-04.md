# Chapter 4: Finite Time

*Pip’s adventure: Pip's calendar has no spare battery. Fictional teaching story; concepts follow the cited source.*

Original learning summary of Tanya Reilly's The Staff Engineer’s Path (2022). Source: text lines 4071–5121.

Pip’s calendar fills while energy, credibility, and relationships run down. Time is only one finite resource. The workshop needs priorities, thinking time, and negotiated scope. Sustainable influence means preserving the capacity to help after this week’s emergency, not promising unlimited effort.

## Resource budget

Pip accepts a presentation after an exhausting incident. The calendar has a gap; the energy budget does not. Projects consume credibility, relationships, quality of life, skills, and social capital as well as hours. A commitment can draw down the resource needed for the next difficult task. Pip asks what this request will cost and how that resource can recover before treating an empty slot as available capacity.

## Prioritization

Pip can do the extra migration work, but should it displace the workshop’s crucial project? Prioritization compares significance, ability to help, constraints, and opportunity cost. A safe slice may fit the quarter where the whole migration does not. Being capable is not enough reason to accept. Pip makes deferred work visible so the team can choose deliberately rather than inherit an impossible queue.

## Thinking time

Pip tries to settle a design dispute in the five minutes between meetings. Nothing clicks. Two protected hours reveal the dependency everyone missed. Deep analysis is work even without an immediate code change or meeting artifact. Fragmenting every hour creates hidden execution risk. Pip reserves uninterrupted attention for difficult choices instead of leaving judgment to whatever scraps the calendar happens to spare.

## Sustainability

The workshop asks for one more emergency favor. Pip explains which existing promise would suffer and offers a smaller scope or another owner. Saying no can protect an important commitment. Sustainable influence means help remains available next month, not just tonight. Pip negotiates the trade-off before credibility is spent on a missed promise; a reliable pace serves the team better than unbounded effort.

## Teaching extension

Pip faces a migration, incident recovery, and a new teammate to onboard. The workshop’s date is fixed, but the migration scope can change. These invented practice choices are not reported incidents from the book. Use the reflection to apply the distinction to your own role.

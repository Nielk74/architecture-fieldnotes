# Chapter 9: What’s Next?

Original learning summary of Tanya Reilly's The Staff Engineer’s Path (2022). Source: text lines 9790–10737.

A career is an ongoing set of choices about learning, environment, values, and scope. The final chapter encourages deliberate investment, honest checks on whether a role still fits, and multiple possible paths including staying, changing roles, or changing employers.

## Investment

Career investment chooses skills, relationships, experiences, and environments that support a desired future. Skills, visibility, relationships, and experiences expand the opportunities available to you. Choose investments that fit what you want next, rather than treating promotion as the only possible objective. Some valuable opportunities require deliberate practice outside your usual responsibilities.

## Fit

Role fit is checked by comparing what the job provides with values, needs, and growth direction. Periodically ask what the role gives you and what it costs. Consider the good parts as well as frustrations. An honest assessment may support staying, renegotiating scope, or moving; there is no single tenure or title that proves you are growing.

## Options

Options include deepening a current role, changing scope, moving organizations, consulting, or management. Different choices offer different forms of learning, impact, stability, and autonomy. You can stay with an employer while changing your work, or move to find something unavailable there. Evaluate the work and environment instead of assuming every upward-sounding title is better.

## Reflection

Reflection turns experience into learning by reviewing outcomes, energy, and the next deliberate experiment. You are responsible for your career choices and for the effects of the software you help create. Revisit what matters to you and what you want to become able to do. A small intentional experiment can reveal more than waiting for a perfect long-term plan.

## Teaching extension

Your current role offers visibility but no time to build distributed-systems depth. A lateral role offers learning with less title recognition. These invented practice choices are not reported incidents from the book. Use the reflection to apply the distinction to your own role.

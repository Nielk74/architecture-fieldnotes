# Chapter 9: What’s Next?

*Pip’s adventure: Which door does Pip want next?. Fictional teaching story; concepts follow the cited source.*

Original learning summary of Tanya Reilly's The Staff Engineer’s Path (2022). Source: text lines 9790–10737.

Pip sees a title and a role offering deeper learning. Career choices involve values, environment, relationships, energy, and scope—not one universal ladder. Reflection and investments help Pip compare staying, changing work, or moving employers, then test a next step.

## Investment

Pip circles a promotion, then notices it offers little practice in the skill they want. Career investment includes skills, visibility, relationships, experiences, and environments that make opportunities possible. Pip reserves time for distributed-systems experiments beyond responsibilities. The right investment depends on the future being sought; promotion is one possible objective, not the only one. Deliberate practice may open a different door.

## Fit

The workshop offers Pip autonomy but leaves little energy for learning. A role-fit review counts the good parts as well as the frustrations: what does the job provide, what does it cost, and how does that compare with values, needs, and growth? Staying, renegotiating scope, or moving may each make sense. Neither a particular title nor time served proves the role still fits.

## Options

Three doors appear on Pip’s sketch: deepen the current role, change scope, or leave the organization. Consulting and management offer other possibilities. Each trades learning, impact, stability, and autonomy differently. Pip could stay with the same employer and change the work, or move for something unavailable there. The useful comparison concerns the actual work and environment, not which door carries the most upward-sounding title.

## Reflection

Pip cannot draw a perfect career map, so chooses a bounded learning experiment and a review date. Reflection turns experience into information: outcomes, energy, values, and what to become able to do next. Pip remains responsible for career choices and the effects of the software they help create. A small deliberate experiment can reveal more than waiting indefinitely for a flawless long-term plan.

## Teaching extension

Pip’s visible workshop role leaves no time for distributed-systems depth. A lateral role offers learning but less title recognition. Which investment fits? These invented practice choices are not reported incidents from the book. Use the reflection to apply the distinction to your own role.

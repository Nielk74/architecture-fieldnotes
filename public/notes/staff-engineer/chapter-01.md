# Chapter 1: What Would You Say You Do Here?

*Pip’s adventure: The badge with three job descriptions. Fictional teaching story; concepts follow the cited source.*

Original learning summary of Tanya Reilly's The Staff Engineer’s Path (2022). Source: text lines 470–1404.

Pip’s new staff-engineer badge arrives with conflicting requests. Writing more code will not settle them. The role gains leverage through technical direction, judgment, and helping teams succeed. Pip compares actual work with the manager’s expectations, making communication, scope, and organizational context part of the technical job.

## Scope

Pip’s badge says “staff,” but three teams read three different promises. Scope is the negotiated boundary of responsibility and influence, not a title’s hidden instruction manual. Pip and the manager write one sentence: which area to serve, which decisions to influence, and which gaps to fill. Another workshop may need a different staff role. The conversation establishes responsibility before the request queue establishes it by accident.

## Judgment

A difficult bug tempts Pip away from an incident review. But colleagues need a decision under uncertainty: rollback or rewrite, with which constraints and consequences? Technical judgment joins the bigger picture, important execution, and helping others become capable. Pip explains why a rollback fits this situation, then helps the team act. Choosing where to contribute is part of the job; the hardest code is not always the most useful work.

## Communication

“Perhaps change the API,” Pip says. By lunch, three teams think that was an order. Influence travels farther than code. Pip replaces the ambiguous remark with a short note: context, suggestion, and the response needed. Questions, writing, and explanation are execution because they move understanding between people. A colleague can now proceed without another meeting—and can tell a possibility from an actual decision.

## Role shape

Pip tries to imitate a celebrated staff engineer and spends the week in the wrong meetings. The workshop needs something different. Reporting chain, scope, preferred work, and primary focus do not always point together. Pip compares the agreed focus with requests arriving this quarter and names the mismatch. A staff role answers local organizational needs; negotiating its shape is more useful than copying someone else’s checklist.

## Teaching extension

Three workshop teams ask Pip for architecture, release help, and mentoring. The manager counts only Pip’s code. Which expectation should guide the work? These invented practice choices are not reported incidents from the book. Use the reflection to apply the distinction to your own role.

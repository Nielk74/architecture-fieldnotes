# Chapter 1: What Would You Say You Do Here?

Original learning summary of Tanya Reilly's The Staff Engineer’s Path (2022). Source: text lines 470–1404.

A staff engineer is a senior individual contributor whose leverage comes from technical direction, judgment, and helping several teams succeed. The opening chapter asks the reader to describe the work they actually do, align expectations with managers, and treat communication and organizational context as part of the technical role.

## Scope

Scope is the boundary of responsibility and influence negotiated with the organization. Agree with your manager on the area you serve, the decisions you are expected to influence, and the gaps you should fill. A title alone does not establish these boundaries, and another company's staff role may be shaped very differently.

## Judgment

Technical judgment means choosing under uncertainty while making constraints and consequences explicit. The role combines seeing a bigger picture, executing important work, and raising the capability of others. Choosing which of these needs your attention is part of the work; doing the hardest coding task yourself is not always the most useful contribution.

## Communication

Communication is part of execution: questions, writing, and explanations move context between people. Your influence extends beyond code you personally change. A clear explanation can help several teams act consistently, while an ambiguous suggestion from a senior person may be mistaken for a decision. Make your intent and the needed action explicit.

## Role shape

A staff role is an organizational need, not a universal checklist; its shape depends on local gaps. Investigate your reporting chain, scope, preferred work, and current primary focus. These can point in different directions. Naming the mismatch lets you negotiate a useful role instead of trying to imitate a famous staff engineer's career.

## Teaching extension

A platform team asks you to own architecture, unblock releases, and mentor three teams, but your manager measures only your own code output. These invented practice choices are not reported incidents from the book. Use the reflection to apply the distinction to your own role.

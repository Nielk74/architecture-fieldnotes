## Recognize costs before a crisis

Pip notices engineers repeatedly spending days reproducing customer failures. Unexplained incidents, long debugging, and rework can reveal a missing production-understanding capability. These costs may be normalized before executives see a catastrophe. Pip builds the case around durable constraints instead of only the latest dramatic outage.

## Measure a bounded change

Pip records one service’s baseline before starting a pilot. Detection and resolution time are useful, alongside severity, rework, responder burden, and independent investigation. Changing averages alone are weak evidence if workload or incidents change too. Pip defines the intended outcome and measures the bounded intervention with context.

Pip buys telemetry storage but the crew has no time to explore it. Observability needs confidence, access, and permission as well as technical capability. Pip funds instrumentation and platform work while protecting curious investigation and reducing punishment for questions. Collecting records is not the same as using them.

## Define a useful enough checkpoint

Pip asks when the observability work is useful enough to reassess. Predictable delivery, sufficient reliability, routine instrumentation, and ordinary investigation habits form a practical checkpoint. Reduced dependence on outside experts and answers to important questions show progress. Pip then targets the next constraint instead of chasing complete coverage for its own sake.

## Transfer challenge: The emergency purchase proposal

After a major outage, leadership proposes buying a platform without allocating adoption time.

### Tie a funded pilot to recurring diagnostic costs

Creates a testable path from investment to useful behavior. Needs baseline work and team participation. The organization can decide whether to expand using evidence from actual team outcomes.

### Purchase immediately and count installed agents

Moves procurement quickly. May leave the original investigation habits and constraints unchanged. A larger telemetry inventory may arrive without a corresponding improvement in diagnosis.

Urgency can justify action, but the business outcome still depends on how the team uses the capability.

## Write an evidence-based investment case

Propose a bounded observability improvement with measurable outcomes and explicit assumptions.

- Recurring business or engineering cost
- Pilot scope and baseline
- Success measure and alternative explanations

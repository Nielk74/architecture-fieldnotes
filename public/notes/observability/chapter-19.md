# 19. The Business Case for Observability

Make the investment case with local outcomes

A business case for observability should explain which recurring costs and limitations the organization intends to change. Waiting for a catastrophe can produce a rushed tool purchase without the practices needed to benefit from it. A proactive case instead connects difficult diagnosis, repeated rework, on-call strain, or uncertain delivery with a bounded improvement effort. Baseline measures help evaluate the result, but incident timings alone do not capture every benefit or establish causality. The chapter also emphasizes permission and support: engineers need time, usable tools, and a culture that encourages investigation. Sufficient observability is a practical checkpoint where teams can deliver and operate predictably, with instrumentation and exploration embedded in routine work rather than sustained by exceptional outside intervention.

## Recognize costs before a crisis

Recurring unexplained failures, prolonged debugging, and repeated rework can indicate that the team lacks an effective way to understand production. These costs may be normalized long before a catastrophic outage makes them visible to executives. Build the case around those existing constraints so the initiative addresses a durable problem rather than only the latest dramatic incident.

## Measure a bounded change

Choose a team or service, record relevant baseline outcomes, and define what an observability pilot should improve. Detection and resolution times are understandable starting measures, but include context such as incident severity, rework, and responder burden. A change in averages alone is weak evidence when the incidents or workload also changed.

## Fund practice as well as infrastructure

A technically observable system can remain poorly understood if engineers lack time, confidence, or permission to explore it. Budget instrumentation and platform work, support curious investigation, and reduce incentives that punish asking questions. Adoption requires the organization to use its capability, not simply collect records or purchase an analytics interface.

## Define a useful enough checkpoint

Observability is never finished in an absolute sense, but investment competes with other needs. A useful checkpoint is predictable delivery and sufficient reliability, with instrumentation and investigation becoming ordinary team habits. Look for reduced dependence on outside experts and the ability to answer important questions, then prioritize the next constraint rather than chase complete coverage as an end itself.

## Apply it

Propose a bounded observability improvement with measurable outcomes and explicit assumptions.

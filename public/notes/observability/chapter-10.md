# 10. Applying Observability Practices in Your Team

Prove value on a problem that already hurts

Introducing observability requires a change in team practice as well as a working tool. The chapter recommends beginning with a painful, elusive production problem where better investigation can demonstrate visible value. A quiet peripheral service may be easy to instrument but offers little opportunity to show why the effort matters. Use an existing solution to shorten the path to evidence, evaluate whether it supports genuine exploration, and share the successful investigation with colleagues. Community participation provides additional experience and comparison, while internal ownership keeps the new capability usable. Adoption grows when people can repeat a useful method on their own work, rather than being asked to accept an abstract promise or a growing telemetry bill.

## Choose a meaningful pain point

A pilot needs a problem that observability can actually help solve. The chapter cautions against choosing an unimportant, already healthy service merely because it seems safe. Instrument a persistent, difficult production issue whose resolution would matter to the team, then use the investigation to demonstrate value that colleagues can recognize in their daily work.

## Shorten the path to value

Building an entire observability platform before testing its usefulness creates a long delay between effort and benefit. The chapter favors trying existing solutions early and judging them by investigative capability. A product label is insufficient: the team should test whether it can ask new questions, preserve context, and find an elusive explanation in its own service.

## Share the method behind a win

A solved incident demonstrates value most effectively when colleagues can see how the result was obtained. Share the instrumentation, comparisons, and reasoning, and make the investigation available to the service’s responders. This turns an individual success into a practice others can repeat instead of creating another expert whose private knowledge becomes essential.

## Learn with a community

Other teams can provide useful comparisons about instrumentation, workflows, and adoption barriers. Community participation works through reciprocal contribution, not only emergency requests for help. Use others’ experiences to question assumptions and design better experiments, while adapting their advice to your own architecture and organizational constraints rather than copying a stack uncritically.

## Apply it

Identify a painful problem and the investigation outcome that would persuade the affected team.

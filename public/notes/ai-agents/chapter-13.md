# Chapter 13: Human-Agent Collaboration

Original learning notes. Source: text lines 12092-12759 in the supplied book.

Human-agent collaboration depends on deciding which work an agent may perform, when it should clarify uncertainty, and when a person must take over. Delegation can begin with suggestions and drafts, then expand as observed performance supports greater independence. A useful handoff includes the current state, evidence, uncertainty, and decision still needed, so the reviewer can continue without reconstructing the whole interaction. Interfaces, training, and stakeholder feedback help people understand both capability and limits. Oversight also needs action records and routes to correction. The goal is calibrated reliance: neither unquestioning acceptance nor rejection of useful automation regardless of evidence.

## Roles and autonomy

Roles and autonomy set who may act: reversible routine work can be delegated, while ambiguous eligibility decisions remain with an accountable human.

## Uncertainty and handoffs

Uncertainty and handoffs preserve continuity when evidence is missing or stakes rise; the agent must state why it stopped and what state the reviewer inherits.

## Stakeholder alignment

Stakeholder alignment calibrates reliance through representative tasks, phased rollout, training, and feedback from users and support teams.

## Oversight and trust

Oversight and trust require auditability, escalation, correction, and undo; observed performance and controls matter more than a convincing demonstration.

# Chapter 28: Accelerating SREs to On-Call and Beyond

Source: text lines 13348–13941 of the supplied book extract.

Training an SRE means developing the ability to reason about unfamiliar failures, not merely learning a list of operating procedures. A cumulative learning path gives newcomers a clear route through the service and gives mentors evidence of progress. Reverse engineering, comparative diagnosis, and postmortem study build models that support improvisation. Disaster role-play and contained hands-on breakages let trainees practice before they carry full incident responsibility. Updating documentation with experts turns gaps into shared learning. Shadow shifts expose real decisions, while reverse shadowing lets a newcomer lead with experienced support. On-call readiness is an important milestone rather than the end of education; growing project ownership and continuing team learning keep both newcomers and experienced engineers effective as production changes.

## An ordered learning path

A training plan should progress through service concepts and practical tasks in a sequence the learner can understand. It can follow the request path, naming relevant documents, expert contacts, and questions that demonstrate comprehension. Mentors assess understanding rather than merely completed reading. This gives newcomers visible progress and helps the team agree on what readiness for operational responsibility actually means.

## Reasoning beyond procedures

Runbooks cannot anticipate every incident, so training should develop reverse engineering, comparison, and hypothesis testing. Learners inspect instrumentation, reconstruct component relationships, and explain what observations support their model. Comparing healthy and unhealthy cases narrows possibilities under pressure. Presenting findings to an expert also reveals outdated assumptions in the team’s existing understanding, making education useful to both parties.

## Practice failures before primary responsibility

Postmortems expose past failure mechanisms; role-play lets a group explain investigation and mitigation choices; contained breakage develops familiarity with real tools. These methods complement one another. A realistic isolated stack can be impaired while learners predict and observe its response, creating experience without making their first genuine page the first time they have diagnosed a failing service.

## Shadowing, documentation, and continuing ownership

Shadow on-call shifts let trainees observe real incidents and discuss decisions afterward without owning the immediate response. Reverse shadowing gives the learner responsibility with an experienced engineer available to help. Pairing newcomers with experts to revise documentation spreads knowledge and builds trust. Project ownership should grow alongside operational skill, while ongoing technical sessions keep the whole team current after qualification.

The lesson’s examples, decision scenario, and exercise are original teaching extensions rather than reported incidents.
